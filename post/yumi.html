Building an MDS agent inside the patient chart

I co-founded Yumi Health to build an electronic health record for skilled nursing. Our Minimum Data Set agent starts with a retrieval pipeline we built in-house, so clinicians can inspect the chart records behind each suggested answer.

Han Wang · CTO and Co-founder, Yumi Health

From the founding team to the first pilot

As CTO and co-founder, I helped recruit the founding engineering team and worked on-site with nursing home clinicians to get our first pilot into their hands. I built Yumi’s first iOS clinical copilot around live voice and the patient chart. It could prepare draft orders, notes, and assessments and ask for missing details before a clinician reviewed what to save.

Co-founding Yumi has meant connecting product direction with hands-on engineering. Working alongside clinicians shaped how I built admission assessments, order sets, and care planning into the platform. Those workflows gave the agent both the clinical context to reason with and a place for clinicians to review its work.

That approach carried into our MDS work. A Minimum Data Set assessment asks clinicians to code a resident’s condition and care from records that may be spread across nursing notes, evaluations, diagnoses, and treatment documentation. I worked on both sides of the agent, from retrieving source records for individual questions to putting the supporting records alongside suggestions in the assessment workflow.

How our home-built retrieval pipeline works

Each MDS question asks about a specific clinical fact and may have its own observation period. The pipeline handles those boundaries before it turns chart material into a suggested answer.

  1. Set the window. Start with the resident, the MDS question, and the Assessment Reference Date, which anchors the question’s observation period.
  2. Find relevant records. Combine clinician tags, rule-based links from structured chart fields, and keyword retrieval over notes. Keep each result linked to its original record.
  3. Work out what the chart supports. Use rules for structured facts and a language model to interpret narrative. Check the proposed answer against the item’s allowed values and show gaps or conflicting records for review.
  4. Show the answer with its source. Present a suggested answer, an explanation, and a link to the chart as Evidence. Relevant material that needs further review appears as a Reference. Clinicians decide what to apply to the assessment.

Why an order and an administration need separate treatment

Consider an oxygen order. It helps the agent find a record relevant to an oxygen-therapy question. For a question about care actually delivered, a dated administration record can support an answer when it falls within the observation period. The order remains available as a Reference. The distinction makes it possible for a reviewer to see which fact supports the suggestion and where documentation is still needed.

The same review workflow lets clinicians inspect how selected answers affect a Medicare case-mix preview before they apply any changes to the assessment.
